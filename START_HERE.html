OUR PROJECT

An educational 2D RPG

Living on Mars.
Learning through choices.

Space exploration depends on people as much as technology.

Ages 7–12Browser-basedScience + empathy + strategy

Sol Zero introduces children to the real-life challenges, daily responsibilities, and psychological pressures faced by astronauts. Players manage a Martian habitat dome, where decisions affect the survival and well-being of the crew.

The project translates aerospace concepts into intuitive gameplay: balancing resources, maintaining life support, handling communication delays with Earth, and caring for crew mood during long missions.

01 · Purpose

What does it do?

  • Simulates daily life and operations inside a Martian dome.
  • Connects life-support resources with environmental conditions.
  • Introduces crew mood, stress, and mental health.
  • Explores delayed communication and tasks involving Earth.
  • Makes space science accessible through interactive play.
02 · Gameplay

How does it work?

  1. Manage the habitat. Oversee daily routines and survival needs.
  2. Balance resources and crew needs. Maintain life support while considering emotional well-being.
  3. Make operational decisions. Respond to events, handle communication tasks, and choose priorities.
03 · Value

What are the benefits?

  • Education: learn space science through actions and consequences.
  • Social learning: practise empathy, teamwork, and problem-solving.
  • Inspiration: build curiosity about STEM and space careers.
  • Accessibility: a lightweight browser experience designed to avoid demanding hardware or complex installation.
04 · Impact

What change do we seek?

Short term: give children an engaging, accessible introduction to human spaceflight and STEM through play.

Long term: inspire future scientists and engineers while showing why adaptability, teamwork, and mental health matter in exploration.

05 · Creativity

The human side of space exploration.

Sol Zero focuses on what it takes to live and work off-Earth. Emotional management, delayed communication, and resource strategy bring human and engineering challenges together in a child-friendly 2D RPG.

Every decision can protect something—and put something else at risk.
06 · Design considerations

What guides our decisions?

  • Age-appropriate pacing: introduce intuitive mechanics gradually, without technical overload.
  • Scientific and psychological realism: adapt astronaut challenges to a Martian scenario while distinguishing real concepts from simplified game rules.
  • Visual clarity: make the effects on resources and crew easy to understand.
  • Technical accessibility: use HTML and browser technology to reduce installation and hardware barriers.

FIVE STAGES · ONE CONNECTED PROCESSBuild, observe, improve, and explain.
  1. 01

    Planning

    Define the learning goal.

    Decide what children should learn, which choices they will face, and the order in which mechanics appear.

    OutputLearning goals + a simple mission map.
  2. 02

    Research

    Give each challenge a basis.

    Use NASA and other reliable sources to understand Mars and astronaut needs. Separate scientific facts from simplified game rules.

    OutputSources linked to mechanics: dust → less solar power.
  3. 03

    Implementation

    Build one step at a time.

    Start with one objective. Add short prompts, clear icons, and visible consequences. Reveal a meter only when its mechanic is introduced.

    Move→Water→Solar power→Harder choices
    OutputA playable prototype.
  4. 04

    Evaluation
    & correction

    Test the game and the learning.

    Check controls, progression, and endings. Observe whether children understand the choices. Fix confusing text, difficulty, and bugs, then test again.

    OutputTest evidence + a clearer, improved version.
    Return to stage 03: improve → test again → keep what works.
  5. 05

    Organic
    demonstration

    On the Space Apps platform.

    Tell the development story with real evidence. Show the original problem, a revision, and a normal playthrough. Explain design choices as they become relevant.

    Problem→Evidence→Before / after→Play
    OutputA clear story of how the game works—and why.
Document as you build.

Keep sketches, source notes, screenshots, and test findings throughout stages 01–04. Use them to show the process naturally in stage 05.
