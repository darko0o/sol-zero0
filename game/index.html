    SOL ZERO · PROTOCOL ARES-7 · NASA-DERIVED SURVIVAL SYSTEMS

    01 — FOOD, VARIETY & MORALE (NASA HRP)

    Three identical rations in a row. Repeated meals reduce acceptance: the third repeat costs 50% more time on the next task. The galley shows the current variety state before you choose. Skipping meals drains morale through the sol.

    02 — WATER RECYCLING (NASA ECLSS)

    Ice is not water yet. The purifier consumes electricity per batch and rejects perchlorate brine. Low purity slows crop growth and raises the cost of every repair that needs water.

    03 — SLEEP OR FINISH THE TASK (NASA HRP)

    Sleep or work. Working through the rest window accumulates fatigue; high fatigue forces a verification step after every repair. Sleeping restores performance but leaves faults open until the next sol.

    04 — DUST STORMS & POWER BUDGET

    The real danger is darkness. Suspended dust and panel deposits slash solar generation. Clean the array, then shed the greenhouse, lab or rover dock before the battery empties.

    05 — OXYGEN AVAILABLE, HAZARDOUS AIR

    Beyond O₂. A CO₂ scrubber fault makes a module lethal even with tanks full. Isolate the module or spend one of the spare lithium-hydroxide cartridges.

    06 — RAD MONITOR & REMS WEATHER

    Two instruments, two jobs. The RAD monitor reports interior and exterior dose plus cumulative exposure. The REMS station only announces weather events: dust storms cut solar, high winds slow EVA, extreme cold raises heating demand. Regions are charted as you cross them.

    07 — SCIENCE PAYLOAD & MACHINE EFFICIENCY

    Calibrate once, work faster. The lab's spectrometer minigame measures five samples. A clean calibration reduces system decay for the rest of the sol.
